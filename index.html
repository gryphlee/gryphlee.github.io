About Me

I am an AI Research Engineer at Kooya Inc in BGC, Taguig, where I lead research and development of a Filipino-focused open foundation-model ecosystem. My work is about moving foundation models from passive language systems toward capable reasoning and action-oriented agents, through post-training, reinforcement learning, and training in verifiable, interactive environments.

My Path

I started in applied AI in 2024 at Silicon Valley Associate, a UAE-based consultancy, building an LLM-driven hiring platform and an AI interviewer backed by a full RAG pipeline. From late 2024 through 2025 I was Enterprise AI Architect at Green Attic Roofing in Chicago (remote), converting manual operations into AI-driven workflows across three departments and serving as the organization's AI adoption lead.

In early 2026 I joined Power4All in Pasig on a six-month engagement as Intelligent Systems Engineer, delivering a company-only local AI model, a centralized RAG knowledge base, and a digital twin of the company's water-treatment plant for simulating chemical dosing before touching the physical plant. That work pulled me toward the modeling side: in June 2026 I joined Kooya as AI Infrastructure & Machine Learning Engineer, fine-tuning open models end to end, running the GPU / Ray / vLLM platform, and building the team's evaluation and red-teaming practice, and in October 2026 I moved into my current research role.

I also come from a background as a confidential intelligence professional, with exposure to security analysis and sensitive operational environments, which still shapes how I think about adversarial testing and secure AI systems. I am a 3rd-year BS Electronics Engineering student at Rizal Technological University in Mandaluyong.

Current Work

At Kooya I am designing the full model-development pipeline, continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, synthetic data generation, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning, and building large-scale datasets for Tagalog, Taglish, Cebuano, Ilocano, Kapampangan, and other Philippine languages. Alongside that, I design evaluation frameworks for language capability, reasoning, coding, cybersecurity, and domain-specific performance.

My research thread is agentic reinforcement learning and Agent-Environment Digital Twins: environments that support state forking, counterfactual experimentation, and simulation-based evaluation, so agents can be trained and tested against verifiable feedback rather than static text. I am also exploring specialized systems for robotics, cybersecurity, coding, disaster response, agriculture, and critical infrastructure, and I lead research-paper development from question formulation and experimental design through implementation, evaluation, and analysis.

  • Philippine languages cost 1.8 to 2× more tokens than English across six open-model tokenizers Pre-registered result

    Stan Lee V. Natividad · Kooya PH AI Research · EXP-TOK-001 · 2026-09-29

    Measures how efficiently the tokenizers of five candidate base models and one reference family encode Philippine languages. On 1,012 aligned FLORES+ sentences, every tokenizer spends 1.77 to 2.04× as many tokens on Filipino, Cebuano, Ilocano, and Waray as on the same English content (Pangasinan 1.45 to 1.54×), while English costs the same 1.24 to 1.27 tokens per word for all of them, so the gap is specific to Philippine languages.   • LoRA, LoRA plus embeddings, or full-parameter continued pretraining for Gemma 4 E4B: throughput on A100 and H100, and the cost structure of the first run Engineering note

  • Per-language training loss at no measurable cost: instrumenting a chunked cross-entropy CPT trainer without changing the run Engineering note

LIKHA: a from-scratch protein language model

  • Homolog-expanded enzyme adaptation of a from-scratch protein language model (LIKHA): per-family gains on lignocellulose enzymes Technical report

    Stan Lee V. Natividad · Kooya PH AI Research · 2026-11-09

    The first enzyme-specialization adapter for LIKHA, trained on 555 reviewed lignocellulose-enzyme sequences, gave only a small perplexity gain and a family tag that behaved as a generic “enzyme-like” switch. This report tests the suspected fix, data. UniRef90 homologs of four rice-straw enzyme families are pulled (3,392 sequences after a benign floor and dedupe, vs 657 reviewed before), split cluster-disjoint with an exact-identity leak check, and used to train a tag-aware LoRA adapter (rank 8, general-protein replay, only four new tag-embedding rows trainable). Held-out enzyme NLL falls with no general-protein forgetting, and the gains concentrate where data was poorest: endo-1,4-β-xylanase perplexity drops 10.6 → 8.1 (~24%), while already well-modelled β-glucosidase moves only 4.7 → 4.5 (~4%), the pooled metric understates this because the easy family dominates it. Homolog expansion is the right lever where it matters; the undertrained 354M base remains the ceiling. Computational only; no wet-lab validation.

  • Representation steering, decodability, and best-of-N generation in a from-scratch protein language model (LIKHA) Technical report

    Stan Lee V. Natividad · Kooya PH AI Research · 2026-11-07

    Reimplements activation steering for a 354M-parameter protein LM and runs a nine-iteration auto-research loop. Three findings: steering controls domain/style but not quality (foldability); foldability is linearly decodable from activations (probe AUROC 0.98; 0.89 on generated sequences) yet not steerable, the decode-vs-control gap is the central result; and the practical quality lever is generation + selection, with a temperature × top-p sweep raising the confident-fold rate from 8% to 37.5% (4.5×) from sampling alone.
